# Council Meeting — Logo Architecture

Status: COMPLETE
Date: 2026-09-10

## Question

Should the brand use a symbolic halo mark as the primary identity?

## Positions

### Steve Jobs
The wordmark is enough. Clarity over ornamentation. A halo is decoration. The name speaks.

### Virgil Abloh
The 3% rule applies. A halo could work as a system element — not the primary identity, but a supporting mark across packaging and tags.

### Shawn Stüssy
Let the wordmark breathe. Organic growth means the name carries itself. Halo feels forced.

### G-Dragon
Mystery matters. A halo is too literal for a brand built on ambiguity. Keep it abstract.

### Samuel Ross
Social commentary doesn't need a halo. The concept of "fallen angels" is stronger without visual literalism.

### Grace Wales Bonner
Cultural storytelling is richer than a symbol. The wordmark carries the narrative weight.

### Yoon Ahn
Jewelry thinking: the wordmark is the piece. A halo is an accessory that doesn't add value.

### Kevin Poon
East-West bridge: wordmark works globally. Halo may not translate across cultures.

### Richie Kul
Grassroots credibility comes from the name, not a symbol. Keep it raw.

## Conflicts

- **Virgil vs. Jobs**: Virgil wants the halo as a supporting element; Jobs says it's unnecessary decoration
- **G-Dragon vs. Everyone**: G-Dragon wants ambiguity; others see the halo as too literal
- **Minority**: Virgil and Richie open to halo as secondary mark, but not primary

## Vote

8 / 10 against using halo as primary identity.

## JARVIS Synthesis

Strong consensus against the halo as primary mark. The wordmark-first approach has universal support. The minority (Virgil) raises a valid point about supporting marks — the halo could work as a secondary system element for packaging and tags. The majority's reasoning is consistent: the name carries the brand.

## Final Decision

Rejected halo as primary identity. Wordmark remains the sole primary identity.
